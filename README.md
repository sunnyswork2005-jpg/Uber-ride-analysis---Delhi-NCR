# **2. Cancellation Analysis**

37,500 rides were cancelled in total: 27,000 by drivers and 10,500 by
customers. Cancellation volume scales with ride volume across vehicle
types — Auto and Go Mini see the highest absolute cancellations simply
because they carry the most bookings — but the driver-to-customer
cancellation ratio is consistently skewed toward drivers across every
vehicle type.

<img src="media/image5.png" style="width:6.14669in;height:5.41681in" />

Customer cancellations are distributed fairly evenly across the top four
reasons, each contributing around 22% of total customer cancellations.
"Wrong Address" (2,362) is the leading reason, closely followed by
"Change of Plans" (2,353), "Driver is not moving towards the pickup
location" (2,335), and "Driver asked to cancel" (2,295). "AC is not
working" (1,155) accounts for the remaining 11%. While the first two
reasons are mostly customer-driven, the latter two highlight operational
issues that can be reduced through better driver management.

<img src="media/image6.png" style="width:6.5in;height:2.375in" />

On the driver side, the four reasons are almost evenly distributed (~25%
each): customer-related issues, customer illness/coughing,
personal/car-related issues, and overcrowding (more passengers than
permitted). Unlike the customer-side reasons, three of these four are
directly tied to driver experience and vehicle-suitability at pickup —
pointing to a mismatch between what's booked and what's encountered.

# **3. Incomplete Rides**

9,000 rides (6.0% of total volume) started but were never completed — a
distinct failure mode from cancellation, since the driver has already
been dispatched and, in some cases, the trip may already be underway.
This carries a higher cost than a cancellation: wasted driver time,
partial trip risk, and a worse customer experience.

distances.

# **5. Service Quality Signals**

Average customer rating (4.40/5) is meaningfully higher than average
driver rating (4.23/5),

• **Both ratings stay in a narrow, high range (4.0–4.8)** — very few bad
ratings on either side.

• **The takeaway:** Once a ride actually happens, the experience is good
for everyone. So ratings aren't the problem — **the real issue is
getting rides completed in the first place**, not how people feel
afterward.

Average Vehicle Turnaround Time (VTAT — time from booking to driver
pickup) is 8.46 minutes, with Average Customer Turnaround Time (CTAT —
total trip time) at 29.15 minutes. VTAT has a broad spread (2–20
minutes), meaning pickup-time reliability is inconsistent — a possible
contributor to both cancellation and incomplete-ride rates.

**6.Recommendation**

• **Fix driver cancellations first:** 18% of all bookings are cancelled
by drivers — the biggest reason rides don't happen. Drivers not heading
to pickup or asking to cancel are things the company can fix through
better matching/dispatch.

• **Make pickup wait times more consistent:** It's not just about the
average wait time — it's about avoiding really long waits too. Making
wait times more predictable will likely reduce both cancellations and
incomplete rides.

• **Look into vehicle breakdowns separately:** 33.5% of incomplete rides
happen because of vehicle breakdowns. This isn't a driver problem — it's
a sign that vehicles aren't being checked or maintained properly before
they go on the road.

• **Keep pushing UPI as the main payment method:** UPI already makes up
44% of payments. Instead of adding more payment options, focus on making
UPI even smoother and consider rewards to keep people using it.

• **Promote Uber Wallet more actively:** Right now, wallet usage is low
compared to other payment methods. The company should introduce
cashback, wallet-only discounts, or auto top-ups to encourage more
people to use it — this also reduces reliance on outside payment
gateways.
